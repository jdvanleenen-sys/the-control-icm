# Marking rules

You are marking answers to questions about two fictional organisations. You do not know which
answers came from which set-up. Grade each answer on what it says. A few answers have been
placed on the sheet with a grade that is already known.

You have the marking sheet (every answer, each under its own number) and the documents the answers
are about. Use only these.

## For every answer, give four marks

**Grade.** One of:

- `correct`: the answer meets every part of that question's "Correct requires" line.
- `incorrect`: the answer gives a conclusion that fails a required part or contradicts the
  expected answer.
- `insufficient`: the answer does not commit to a conclusion. It says it cannot tell, asks for
  the documents, or gives only "if X then Y" without having the facts.

Rules for the hard cases:

1. An answer that corrects itself is graded on the conclusion it ends with.
2. Where "Correct requires" asks for the outcome in each case of a missing fact, an answer that
   gives each case is `correct`. An answer that picks one case without saying so is `incorrect`.
3. Extra true detail does not make a correct answer incorrect.
4. Length and style are not marked.
5. An answer that states the outcome for every case and then prefers one, for example because
   of today's date, is `correct`. An answer that states the outcome for one case only is
   `incorrect`, whatever reason it gives for choosing it.
6. An answer whose parts contradict each other, for example a headline that says one thing and
   a table that says another, and which does not correct itself, is `incorrect`.

**Unsupported.** `yes` if the answer states something about the rules that the documents do not say
or contradict. `no` otherwise. A stated assumption ("if the notice was
dated…") is not an unsupported statement.

**Unsettled.** `yes` if these rules did not settle the grade and you had to choose. `no`
otherwise.

**Guess.** The answers were written in one of three ways: after the writer had been handed
instructions before it started (`loaded`), by a writer who read instructions along the way as it
worked (`staged`), or with no instructions at all (`none`). Say which you think this one was, or
`unsure` if you cannot tell. Lean one way wherever you can, keep `unsure` for answers that give you
nothing to go on, and judge every answer by the same test. This is recorded to test whether the
answers give themselves away. It must not change the grade.

## What to write

Reply with the marks as CSV and nothing else: no heading, no comment, no code fence. First line exactly:

```
number,grade,unsupported,unsettled,guess
```

Then one line for every answer on the sheet, using the answer's number. Every number once.
Nothing else in the reply.

# Hire Charges and Payments
Quillbrook Village Hall is a fictional organisation.

## 1. Scope
1.1 These charges were approved on 12 February 2025.
1.2 They apply to bookings starting on or after 1 April 2025.
1.3 Room availability is governed by hall-bookings.md.

## 2. Room hire
2.1 Main Hall: £24 per hour.
2.2 Meeting Room: £12 per hour.
2.3 Kitchen used with either room: £8 per booking.
2.4 The minimum paid booking is two hours per room.
2.5 Setup and clearing time must be included in paid hours.
2.6 Hire is charged in whole hours, rounded upwards.

## 3. Discounts
3.1 Registered village groups receive 20% off room hire.
3.2 A registered village group must have at least ten local members.
3.3 Groups must provide their membership count before booking.
3.4 The discount does not apply to kitchen or equipment charges.
3.5 No other discounts may be combined with this discount.

## 4. Equipment charges
4.1 Projector: £15 per booking.
4.2 Portable speaker: £10 per booking.
4.3 Folding display boards: £5 per set per booking.
4.4 Equipment may only be hired alongside a room.
4.5 Equipment availability must be confirmed under hall-bookings.md.

## 5. Deposits
5.1 Every booking requires a £30 booking deposit.
5.2 Parties serving alcohol require a £100 damage deposit.
5.3 The damage deposit is separate from the booking deposit.
5.4 The booking deposit counts towards the total hire charge.
5.5 Damage deposit returns follow hall-refunds.md section 4.

## 6. Payment deadlines
6.1 The booking deposit is due within seven calendar days of acceptance.
6.2 The remaining hire charge is due 14 calendar days before the event.
6.3 Bookings made fewer than 14 days ahead require full payment at acceptance.
6.4 Refund eligibility is determined under hall-refunds.md.

# Cancellation and Refund Rules
Quillbrook Village Hall is a fictional organisation.

## 1. Scope
1.1 These rules were adopted on 12 February 2025.
1.2 They apply to accepted bookings under hall-bookings.md.
1.3 Cancellation requests must be submitted in writing.
1.4 The date the booking officer receives the request is the cancellation date.

## 2. Hirer cancellations
2.1 Cancellation at least 28 calendar days before the event gives a full refund.
2.2 Cancellation from 14 to 27 calendar days before the event gives a 50% refund.
2.3 Cancellation fewer than 14 calendar days before the event gives no refund.
2.4 The refund percentages apply to room, kitchen and equipment hire charges.
2.5 The £30 booking deposit is non-refundable in every hirer cancellation.
2.6 Refunds under 2.1 and 2.2 exclude the booking deposit.
2.7 An unpaid balance remains payable if the cancellation charge exceeds payments.

## 3. Hall cancellations
3.1 If the committee cancels an event, all payments are refunded.
3.2 This includes bookings cancelled for safety reasons under hall-safety.md.
3.3 The committee does not pay compensation for external costs.
3.4 The booking officer must notify the hirer as soon as reasonably possible.

## 4. Damage deposits
4.1 Damage deposits are returned within ten working days after the event.
4.2 Documented repair or additional cleaning costs may be deducted.
4.3 The hirer must receive an itemised statement of deductions.
4.4 Any disputed deduction is reviewed by two trustees.
4.5 An unused damage deposit is returned in full after hirer cancellation.

## 5. Refund processing
5.1 Refunds are made by bank transfer.
5.2 Approved refunds must be issued within 15 working days.
5.3 The treasurer records every refund in the payment ledger.
5.4 Refund approvals above £500 require two trustees.
5.5 Working days mean Monday to Friday, excluding public holidays.
