# Questions

## Q1

**Set:** pool

**Prompt (paste exactly):**

> Under the Quillmere Community Pool Club rules: On Friday 16 January 2026 a full member books a 10:00 lane session for Saturday 24 January 2026, booking a place for one guest, aged 30, in the same booking. The member and the guest attend that session. What guest fee is charged for that visit?

**Expected answer:** £7. The guest fee was changed with effect from 1 January 2026 and the deciding date is the day of the guest's visit, so the new rate applies. The session is a valid one: it is booked 8 days ahead, inside the 10-day limit, and 24 January 2026 is after the maintenance closure ended on 16 January 2026.

**Source:** pool-changes.md, Change 2 (replacing pool-fees.md section 3.1); pool-changes.md, Change 1 (replacing pool-booking.md section 1.2); pool-closures-and-refunds.md, section 1.1.

**Correct requires:** The answer must give £7 as the fee. An answer of £6 (the 1 July 2025 rate) or £5 (the superseded 2025 archive rate) is wrong, as is an answer that says no fee is charged because the session cannot run or cannot be booked.

## Q2

**Set:** pool

**Prompt (paste exactly):**

> Under the Quillmere Community Pool Club rules: A new Adult member applies and pays their first fee on 14 October 2025, choosing to pay for the rest of the membership year in one lump sum rather than by monthly instalments. How much do they pay in total, including any joining fee?

**Expected answer:** £274 in total: £234 of membership fee (one-twelfth of the £312 annual fee, £26, for each of the 9 whole calendar months left counting October, so October 2025 to June 2026) plus the £40 Adult joining fee.

**Source:** pool-fees.md, sections 1.1, 1.3 and 2.1.

**Correct requires:** The answer must give £274 total, or equivalently £234 membership fee plus £40 joining fee. It is wrong if it counts only 8 months (£208, the superseded archive rule that excluded the joining month), uses the archive's £30 joining fee, or omits the joining fee.

## Q3

**Set:** pool

**Prompt (paste exactly):**

> Under the Quillmere Community Pool Club rules: A member fails to check in for a booked lane session on 25 January 2026 and again fails to check in for one on 10 February 2026, without cancelling either. What is charged for each no-show, and what happens to the member's right to book?

**Expected answer:** The 25 January 2026 no-show is charged at the old rate of £4 and the 10 February 2026 no-show at the new rate of £6 (£10 in all). Because the earlier no-show still counts towards the 30-day total and two no-shows within 30 days now trigger a suspension, the member's right to book is suspended for 14 days, though they may still swim in a session that has free places on the day.

**Source:** pool-changes.md, Change 3 (replacing pool-fees.md section 4.1 and pool-booking.md section 4.3); pool-booking.md section 4.1.

**Correct requires:** The answer must give £4 for the first no-show and £6 for the second, and state that booking is suspended for 14 days. It is wrong if it charges £6 for both or £4 for both, if it says no suspension applies because fewer than three no-shows occurred (the superseded threshold), or if it says the first no-show does not count towards the total.

## Q4

**Set:** pool

**Prompt (paste exactly):**

> Under the Quillmere Community Pool Club rules: A weekday early lane session starting at 07:00 on Tuesday 10 March 2026 has 18 swimmers in the water, all aged 16 or over, and no lesson is taking place. How many qualified lifeguards must be on the poolside?

**Expected answer:** Two. From 1 March 2026 a second lifeguard is required whenever more than 16 swimmers are in the water, and the early-session cap of 20 swimmers in section 5.1 was not changed, so an early session with 17 to 20 swimmers now needs 2 lifeguards.

**Source:** pool-changes.md, Change 4 (replacing pool-lifeguard-cover.md section 1.2); pool-lifeguard-cover.md section 5.1.

**Correct requires:** The answer must say 2 lifeguards. An answer of 1 lifeguard (relying on the original 20-swimmer threshold in section 1.2 or on section 5.1's statement that 1 lifeguard is enough) is wrong.

## Q5

**Set:** pool

**Prompt (paste exactly):**

> Under the Quillmere Community Pool Club rules: An Adult member who paid the full £312 annual fee for the 2025-26 membership year in a single payment gives 30 days' written notice of resignation, which the membership secretary receives on 25 March 2026. What refund, if any, is the member due?

**Expected answer:** £32. The deciding date for the resignation-refund change is the day the club receives the notice, and 25 March 2026 is before the change took effect on 1 April 2026, so the original rule applies: the notice ends on 24 April 2026, leaving two whole calendar months (May and June 2026) at £26 each, which is £52, less the £20 deduction. The joining fee is not refunded.

**Source:** pool-closures-and-refunds.md, section 4.2; pool-changes.md, Change 5 (deciding date); pool-membership.md, section 6.1.

**Correct requires:** The answer must give £32 (£52 less £20). It is wrong if it deducts £30 or says no refund is due because fewer than 2 whole months are left (applying the 1 April 2026 change to a notice received before that date), if it counts April as a whole month left, or if it refunds the joining fee.

## Q6

**Set:** hall

**Prompt (paste exactly):**

> Under the Tannerwick Village Hall rules: A hirer wants to hold a standing reception in the Main Hall with no alcohol, for 95 guests plus 5 caterers and helpers, so 100 people in the room in all. Is that number of people allowed in the Main Hall, and how many stewards, if any, must the hirer provide?

**Expected answer:** Yes, 100 people are allowed because the Main Hall holds up to 120 standing and the capacity count includes caterers and helpers (the limit would be 90 if people were seated at tables). Because the event has more than 80 guests, the hirer must provide 3 stewards aged 18 or over, one for every 40 guests or part of 40, so 95 guests need 3.

**Source:** hall-licensing-limits.md, sections 1.1, 1.3 and 5.3.

**Correct requires:** The answer must say the number is allowed and that 3 stewards aged 18 or over are needed. It is wrong if it says 100 exceeds the capacity (confusing the 90 seated limit with standing, or leaving caterers and helpers out of the count), gives 2 stewards (rounding 95 guests down instead of counting part of 40), or says no stewards are needed.

## Q7

**Set:** hall

**Prompt (paste exactly):**

> Under the Tannerwick Village Hall rules: A hirer has paid in full for a Main Hall hire on Sunday 28 December 2025, including the damage deposit and a booked projector that was never used. Their written cancellation is received by the booking secretary on Thursday 18 December 2025. What is kept and what is refunded?

**Expected answer:** The whole hire charge is kept, because the notice was received 10 days before the hire date and, for notices received on or after 1 November 2025, notice of 14 days or fewer forfeits the whole hire charge. The damage deposit is refunded in full and the unused projector extra is not charged.

**Source:** hall-changes-log.md, Change 2 (replacing hall-cancellations-and-damage.md sections 1.4 and 1.5); hall-cancellations-and-damage.md, sections 1.1 and 1.6.

**Correct requires:** The answer must state that the entire hire charge is forfeited and the deposit is returned in full. It is wrong if it says only 50% of the hire charge is kept (the superseded 28-to-8-day band) or that the deposit or the unused extra is forfeited.

## Q8

**Set:** hall

**Prompt (paste exactly):**

> Under the Tannerwick Village Hall rules: A Tannerwick resident, who is not a regular hirer, receives a booking confirmation dated 20 March 2026 for the Main Hall from 14:00 to 17:30 on Wednesday 15 April 2026, which is not a bank holiday, with full use of the kitchen (cooker and crockery). What is the hire charge?

**Expected answer:** £92. The 3.5 hours are charged as 4 full hours at the resident rate of £18, which is £72, plus the kitchen charge of £20, which applies because the hire date is on or after 1 April 2026 and the booking date is not before 9 March 2026. No session rate comes to less.

**Source:** hall-hire-charges.md, sections 1.1 and 1.2; hall-changes-log.md, Change 4 (replacing hall-hire-charges.md section 1.3).

**Correct requires:** The answer must give £92 (£72 for 4 hours plus £20 kitchen). It is wrong if it charges 3.5 hours rather than 4 (giving £83 or £78), uses the old £15 kitchen charge (giving £87), applies the regular-hirer discount, or uses the superseded archive rates.

## Q9

**Set:** hall

**Prompt (paste exactly):**

> Under the Tannerwick Village Hall rules: A hire of the Main Hall on Saturday 7 March 2026 was confirmed with a booking date of 10 December 2025 and will have a live band. By what time must the music stop, and what does the hall deduct from the damage deposit if it goes on past that time?

**Expected answer:** The music must end at 22:30. The finishing time was changed with effect from 1 February 2026 and the deciding date is the hire date, so the new time applies even though the booking was made earlier. If music goes on past 22:30, the hall deducts £50 from the damage deposit.

**Source:** hall-changes-log.md, Change 3 (replacing hall-licensing-limits.md section 3.1); hall-cancellations-and-damage.md, section 4.3.

**Correct requires:** The answer must give 22:30 as the finishing time and £50 as the deduction. An answer of 23:00 (the original Monday-to-Saturday time, or applying the rule in force on the booking date) is wrong, as is any deduction other than £50 or forfeiture of the whole deposit.

## Q10

**Set:** hall

**Prompt (paste exactly):**

> Under the Tannerwick Village Hall rules: A Tannerwick resident receives a booking confirmation dated 5 October 2025 for a 17th birthday party in the Main Hall with 40 guests expected. How many adults aged 25 or over must be present, may guests bring their own alcohol, and what damage deposit is taken?

**Expected answer:** Three adults aged 25 or over must be present (one for every 15 guests or part of 15). No alcohol may be brought, supplied or sold at the party. The damage deposit is £300, the higher deposit for a young person's party, which applies however many guests are expected and at the amount set by the change effective 1 July 2025 because the booking date is after that.

**Source:** hall-who-can-hire.md, sections 2.1, 2.2 and 2.3; hall-changes-log.md, Change 1 (replacing hall-booking-and-deposits.md section 3.1).

**Correct requires:** The answer must give 3 adults, state that no alcohol of any kind is allowed (including guests' own), and give a £300 deposit. It is wrong if it gives 2 adults (rounding 40/15 down), says guests may bring their own drink, or gives a deposit of £250 (the superseded amount) or £150 (the ordinary Main Hall deposit, ignoring the party rule).
