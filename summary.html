Does an ICM workspace beat the folder?

The competition entry tested a five-file folder against the same documents with no instructions. This follow-up adds a fourth set-up, an ICM workspace built by the ICM method, and runs all four on the same 18 documents, the same model and the same rule.

The ICM was the most accurate set-up: right on all 54 answers by both markers. It also read the most. By the rule fixed before the run, it did not earn its place.

The verdicts, by the rule fixed before the run

The same three-part rule as the competition entry: a set-up earns its place only if it finds the documents in clearly fewer steps, loses no right answers, and puts no more than 5% more text in front of the model.

Did not earn its place
ICM against the documents alone

Fewer steps on 16 of 18 questions and no answers lost. It failed on text alone: 17,781 characters against 8,171.

Did not earn its place
ICM against the folder

More steps to find the documents (more on 14 of 18 questions) and more text: 17,781 characters against 10,447.

Could not tell
Reading on demand against loading it all first

The same ICM files, loaded when needed instead of all at once, saved 39% of the text and changed nothing else: same answers, about the same steps.

The all-at-once version still opened its two instruction files in almost every session, although they were already loaded.

The rule only checks that a set-up does not lose right answers; it gives no credit for gaining them. The ICM's extra accuracy does not count toward earning its place.

What it means

  • The ICM bought accuracy with reading. Its contract tells the model to read every paper that touches the question, check every change, and show its working before answering. It got every answer right, and it wrote answers almost four times as long as the folder's.
  • The folder is still the cheapest way to the documents. Its map took the model to the right papers in 2.46 steps, and the entry's own result came back in this batch: fewer steps on 10 of 10 of the entry's questions in both sets, with more text and fewer complete answers than the documents alone.
  • On-demand loading is real, and small here. At 18 documents the saving was text, not behaviour. The method places its saving against libraries many times this size.
  • Most questions did not separate the set-ups. On 12 of 18, every set-up was right every time. The differences come from six questions.
  • How it fits the ICM paper. Jake Van Clief and David McDermott's paper says that no controlled comparison of staged loading against loading everything at once has been done yet. This is a small first one, at a scale where the paper would not expect a quality difference, and there was none. The paper's case is at 30,000 to 50,000 tokens.

How it was kept fair

Questions written first18 new questions written by a separate Claude Opus 5.5 session from the documents alone, key-checked by another model, committed before the ICM existed. I never read them until the run was over.
The ICM built blindBuilt by the icm-architect method on practice documents only, in the house style of my PO Verifier. The builder never saw the live documents or questions.
Practice matchedThe ICM got the same five practice rounds the folder had, then had to pass two gate audits by sessions that did not build it.
Plan reviewed four timesThree design reviews (method, ICM fidelity, a skeptic), then one by Perplexity, which added the all-at-once set-up. Every change is logged.
Markers blind, two makersAnswers shuffled under random numbers. Four planted answers of known grade were marked right by both. Neither marker could guess the set-up (44% and 32%).
One sealed run, every session kept456 sessions, no faults. A first run was voided when a usage limit refused a quarter of its sessions; it is published, never analysed.
The verdict is a scriptThe rule, the thresholds and the sentences were code, tested on made-up data before the run. It was run once.
Predictions on recordWritten before the run: fewer steps than the documents alone, and more text than both. Both held.

What this cannot tell you

  • Anything about ICM's hand-offs between stages or its human checks. For a single question the method chose one stage: there is nothing to hand on and nobody to check it.
  • Anything about large libraries, where ICM claims its biggest saving. This one is about 32,000 characters.
  • Anything about every possible ICM. This one passed every check and two audits, but its one stage contract is about three times the size the method recommends. Its instruction to read every relevant paper and show the working most likely bought both the extra accuracy and the extra cost.
  • Anything beyond one model and made-up rule documents. The new questions were written and checked by Claude models.
